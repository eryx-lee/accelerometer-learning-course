Why this course

Build the evidence trail—not just the output.

Accelerometers record acceleration, not ready-made measures of activity, sedentary behavior, or sleep. Credible results depend on the decisions made before, during, and after data collection.

This course connects those decisions into one practical workflow: define the protocol, preserve traceability, process raw files, interrogate data quality, and deliver a dataset another analyst can understand. Examples use ActiGraph, GGIR in R, and Stata, while the underlying principles extend beyond any one tool.

Quick summary Brief review

This course follows accelerometer data from study planning and device deployment through downloading, organization, GGIR processing, quality control, cleaning, and creation of an analysis-ready Stata dataset. Throughout the workflow, study-specific decisions and reproducible documentation are treated as essential.

Getting started

Who is this course for?

It is designed for undergraduate and graduate students; research assistants, coordinators, and laboratory teams; faculty, instructors, and investigators; public-health, clinical, exercise, and movement professionals; data analysts and methodologists; and company or consulting teams working in wearable technology, digital health, occupational health, or research services. The concept route is beginner-friendly, while the hands-on route requires appropriate software, devices, and safe practice data.

How long does the course take?

Allow about 3–4 hours for the concept route or 6–9 hours for the hands-on route. Full-dataset processing, software installation, and protocol approval take additional time. Each module card shows a planning estimate.

Which devices and software does the course use?

The examples use ActiGraph devices, ActiLife, GGIR in R, and Stata. You can complete the concept route in a browser. Hands-on work requires appropriate software and a safe practice dataset; access to ActiLife and Stata may depend on your laboratory or institution’s licensing.

Do I need previous experience with R, GGIR, or Stata?

No prior experience is required for the concept route. For the hands-on route, you should be comfortable finding files, editing paths, running a script, reading software messages, and protecting participant data. Basic familiarity with R and Stata is helpful in Modules 4–7.

How can I use the videos accessibly?

Each video is followed by a quick summary, a full transcript, and a downloadable caption track. The summary is for rapid review; it is not a substitute for the transcript or captions. Software controls and visual-only details are described in the accompanying text where they affect the task.

How do I know that I completed a module?

Complete the self-check and compare your reasoning with the feedback. On the hands-on route, also save the module’s stated deliverable—for example, a protocol decision, download log, GGIR configuration, QC log, or analysis script.

Methods and files

What does an accelerometer measure directly?

It records acceleration over time, usually along three axes. Behaviors such as activity intensity, sedentary time, posture, and sleep are estimates derived from the raw signal using a documented algorithm, wear location, and protocol.

Does GGIR require conversion from .gt3x to .csv?

No. GGIR can read .gt3x files directly. Convert to CSV only when a downstream script, another software package, data-sharing requirement, or audit workflow specifically needs it.

What is the difference between .gt3x and .agd files?

A .gt3x file contains raw ActiGraph acceleration data. The demonstrated workflow also creates a 10-second .agd, a count-based epoch summary that may be useful for ActiLife review or legacy workflows. Follow the approved protocol if it specifies another epoch length.

What counts as a valid participant record?

The course example uses at least 4 valid days, at least 10 hours of awake wear per valid day, and at least 1 weekend day. These are example rules and must be approved for the study population, device, placement, and protocol.

Are the course thresholds and cleaning rules universal?

No. Device settings, valid-day rules, intensity thresholds, wear criteria, and variable-specific cleaning rules are study-specific examples. Confirm every rule against the approved protocol, the exact software version, and the relevant output dictionary before applying it.

What should the final analysis package contain?

Include a dated .dta file, data dictionary, cleaning log, exclusion table, Stata do-file, and a README that identifies the source GGIR run and processing batches.
